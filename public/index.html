Case study 01

MADA

C2C circular fashion · Gulf region · 2026 · End-to-end UX/UI, Double Diamond

The problem

The Gulf has no dedicated fashion resale platform — the "Vinted effect" simply never arrived. Up to 90% of textiles end up in landfill for lack of collection and resale systems, while generic classifieds like Dubizzle and Haraj feel cluttered and unsafe for fashion.

Research

  • Desk research, netnography across Reddit, X and the App Store, plus a survey
  • 78% of Saudi e-commerce happens on a smartphone
  • 64% try to sell unused clothes — the top barrier is fear of scams
  • 66% rate app aesthetics as "very important"
  • 58% of GCC consumers seek sustainable emotional gratification

Who it's for

Sara, 32 — Bahrain, marketing. Wants to sell fast and safely; fears not getting paid.

Zaid, 22 — Saudi Arabia, tech student. Tired of dated classifieds, wants real filters and privacy.

Design response

  • Escrow payments — money held until delivery is confirmed
  • Protected in-app chat, so no phone number is ever shared
  • Fashion-specific filters: size, brand, condition
  • Integrated home pickup and delivery logistics
  • Premium minimalist UI to strip the "cheap secondhand" stigma

Palette & type

#2E5BFF Electric Blue — CTAs and payment states, reads as technical security #F9F8F3 Alabaster White — gallery-like backdrop for the product grid #231F20 Charcoal Black — text and overlays; premium without harsh pure black

The palette does the heavy lifting on the core problem: users confirmed it removes the "used clothing" stigma and reads as a premium boutique.

Case study 02

TripMate

Collaborative travel planning · 2025 · UX research, IA, prototyping

The problem

Travellers juggle one app for packing, another for bookings, another for activities and notes. Things get forgotten, time gets lost, and what should be exciting turns stressful before the trip even starts.

Research

  • Four personas built from the distinct ways people travel
  • Competitive teardown of PackPoint, TripIt and Tripsy
  • PackPoint — great packing generator, weak itineraries, no collaboration
  • TripIt — auto-imports bookings, but has no packing checklist at all
  • Tripsy — nice UI, but the packing list sits behind the paywall
  • Mind map and sitemap before wireframes, then high-fidelity Figma

Who it's for

Maria, 27 — busy professional; needs a trip set up in minutes.

Daniel, 24 — first-time traveller; doesn't know what to pack and fears losing documents.

Carla, 39 — packs for a family of four and shares the trip with her husband.

Alex, 28 — adventure seeker; needs it to work with no signal.

Design response

  • Step-by-step trip creation: destination, dates, activities
  • Packing checklist generated from destination, duration and weather
  • Multi-bag organisation with visual packing progress
  • Budget tracker with a spending cap
  • Share a trip by link, and full offline access

Palette & type

#6AE8A7 Mint — primary actions and trip cards, the app's signature #5DCB92 Deep Mint — pressed and secondary states #000000 Black — date pills and labels, maximum contrast on mint #B8DAF5 Sky — drawn from the destination photography, not a fixed brand colour

Full-bleed destination photography carries the emotion; the mint and black UI sits on top of it without competing.

Case study 03

Kebab Gourmet

Ordering website · Espartinas, Sevilla · 2026 · Design through to deployment

The problem

A neighbourhood kebab shop with no storefront of its own: orders arrived by phone or through Glovo, Just Eat and Uber Eats, each taking a cut and owning the customer relationship. It needed a direct channel that its regulars would actually use.

Research

  • Constraints taken from the business itself: its real menu, two phone lines, split opening hours and the platforms it already sold on
  • WhatsApp identified as the channel the shop already ran orders through, so the design had to meet it there rather than replace it
  • Unlike MADA and TripMate, this one has no formal discovery deck — the requirements came from the operation, not from a research phase

Who it's for

The customer — hungry, one-handed on a phone, deciding in under a minute. Not browsing, not comparing.

The shop — needs the order to land somewhere it already watches during service, without new software to learn mid-rush.

Design response

  • One thumb-reachable flow: pick, customise, order
  • Extras and sauces as chips, never dropdowns — no hidden choices
  • Persistent cart and an order button that never leaves the screen
  • Delivery or pickup toggle with an address form
  • WhatsApp as a first-class ordering route, not a fallback afterthought
  • Honest hand-off to Glovo, Just Eat and Uber Eats for those who prefer them

Palette & type

#F97316 Orange — the brand colour, reserved for "Pedir Ahora" #16A34A Green — confirm and checkout, distinct from the brand CTA #F0FDF4 Mint Wash — selected states and price chips #1A1A1A Near-black — body copy, softer than pure black on white

Type: Karla for the interface, Bebas Neue for the discount figures — condensed so a "50%" lands hard without stealing the CTA's colour.
